## 1. Wedge offer

**Lead with: AI voice agent (missed-call answering), entered through a free AI opportunity audit.**

Why, for Perth tradies and small operators:
- Pain is felt and obvious. A tradie on the tools cannot answer the phone; the caller rings the next business. `PRODUCT.md` frames the audience as owners dealing with "missed enquiries" and "repetitive administration". No stat needed to say it; ask them how many calls they miss.
- It is demonstrable in 30 seconds. Good'Ai already has a demo line, (08) 7741 4191 (`lib/links.ts`), and a browser demo at `/demo`. Prospect rings it from their own phone. Nothing else in the five services sells itself that fast.
- Narrow scope = fast delivery. `lib/services.ts` voice scope: enquiry capture, common questions, human hand-off. Booking/CRM links are "quoted separately", so the first build can stay small.
- The audit is the free door, not the product. It costs Kevin time, not money, produces a written plan, and converts the doubtful ("I don't know if I need this") into a scoped voice or automation job. `lib/services.ts` already says audit savings are "planning assumptions to validate, not promised outcomes". Use that exact stance.

Sequence:
1. Free 20-minute audit call (door opener).
2. Voice agent pilot (the first paid job).
3. Business automation upsell (quote follow-up, reminders) once the voice agent is live, because the agent produces the leads that need chasing.

Park for now: custom assistants and AI integration projects. Long scoping, higher risk, bad fit for a cash-strapped first 10. Mention only if asked.

Honest positioning for the first clients: Good'Ai has no case studies yet. Say so. Offer an early-client arrangement (see Option A below) and only quote results that actually happen, with the client's written OK.

Target segments, in order (all within reach of Perth in person):
1. Trades with 1-5 staff: plumbers, electricians, air-con, locksmiths, cleaners, landscapers.
2. Appointment-based micro-businesses: mobile mechanics, pet groomers, physios, hair/beauty.
3. Small property/strata managers and tradie-adjacent suppliers.

## 2. Pricing OPTIONS (for Kevin to decide; do not publish)

All dollar figures are proposals Kevin sets, not market claims. Costs to check before choosing: Trillet usage per minute, Resend, any phone number rental, and Kevin's hours. Rule of thumb: price the pilot so it covers tool costs and Kevin's setup hours even at a discount.

**Option A — Founding-client pilot (fastest to first cash)**
- Free audit. Voice agent setup at a heavily reduced one-off fee plus a small monthly fee for running and tweaking. Month-to-month, cancel any time.
- In return: written permission to describe the setup (and, only if real and approved, the results) in a short case note.
- Proposed placeholders: setup `$___`, monthly `$___`. Limit to the first 3 clients; then move to Option B.
- Pro: lowest objection, produces the first real proof. Con: lowest margin; caps at 3 so it does not become the standing price.

**Option B — Standard productised package**
- One fixed setup fee for a single voice agent with one defined call flow and human hand-off, plus a monthly fee that covers usage up to an agreed allowance and light support.
- Extras (booking links, CRM, SMS follow-up) quoted separately, matching `lib/services.ts` "advanced integrations are quoted separately".
- Placeholders: setup `$___`, monthly `$___`, allowance `___ minutes`.
- Pro: simple to explain, repeatable, healthy margin. Con: asks for real money before Kevin has proof.

**Option C — Audit-first paid sprint**
- Paid opportunity audit (written plan, ordered recommendations, first project scoped), with the fee credited against the first build if they proceed within an agreed window.
- Placeholder: audit `$___`, credit window `___ days`.
- Pro: cash on day one, filters tyre-kickers, stays honest to the audit scope. Con: harder cold sell with no proof; many tradies will say no to paying for advice.

Recommendation to consider: run Option A for clients 1-3 (proof beats margin), then Option B for 4-10, using Option C only for operators with several moving parts. Kevin decides.

Billing hygiene: invoice before go-live for setup; direct debit or card for the monthly. Confirm GST registration status before quoting.

## 3. 14-day zero-dollar outreach plan

Daily time budget: about 2-3 hours. Targets are effort targets, not forecasts; no result is promised.

**Day 0 / prep (do first, one sitting)**
- Claim and finish the Google Business Profile for Good'Ai: category, service area Perth, phone, hours, a plain description from `lib/services.ts` wording. Add 3-4 photos you own (real, not stock). Do not post fake reviews; ask real people only when real work exists.
- Confirm the site is live, `/contact` works and the demo line answers. Ring it yourself from a mobile.
- Make a single list (sheet) of 60 target businesses: name, suburb, phone, public business email, how found, status. Source only from public listings (Google Maps, Yellow Pages, own website).
- Wash the call list against the Do Not Call Register via ACMA's wash service before any calls.
- Personal LinkedIn headline: "I build AI phone answering and admin automation for Perth small businesses". About section: the same, plain.

**Days 1-2: warm first (people who already know Kevin)**
- Personal message to 15 contacts: friends, ex-colleagues, family who own or work in a small business, or know one (script 5 below). Ask for introductions, not sales.
- Post once in your LinkedIn feed: what you built and the free audit offer. No claims about results.

**Days 3-5: local Facebook groups**
- Join 4-6 Perth groups: small business, tradies, and suburb community groups. Read the rules; many ban promotion. Post only where allowed or ask an admin first.
- Post script 6 (value-first, no hard sell) in allowed groups. Reply to every comment within the hour. Never DM people who did not respond or opt in.
- Offer to answer questions about missed calls and quote follow-up in the comments; that is how the audit offers arrive.

**Days 4-8: walk-ins and cold calls**
- Walk into 5-8 trade-adjacent shops per day (trade supply counters, local workshops, salons, groomers, cafes with owner on site). Short chat (script 3 adapted), leave a one-line card with the demo number. Respect "no soliciting" signs.
- Cold call 10-15 numbers per day from the washed list, inside permitted hours (see compliance). Open with script 2; ask for permission before saying more.

**Days 5-10: LinkedIn and email**
- 10 targeted connection requests per day to Perth owner-operators. Message only after they accept (script 1, LinkedIn version).
- Email only business addresses that are publicly published, relevant to the role, and carry no "no unsolicited marketing" notice. Include sender identity and a working unsubscribe line (script 1b). Keep a suppression list; honour opt-outs within 5 business days.

**Days 6-14: audits and follow-up**
- Book and run audits (script 4). Same-day written summary to each (one page: what you heard, what's worth fixing, what to leave alone, what a pilot would involve).
- Follow-up ladder (script 3): once at 3 days, once at 8 days, then stop. A third unrequested message is not allowed.
- Ask every audit contact, win or lose, for one referral (script 5).

**Day 14 review**
- Count: contacts, replies, audits booked, audits done, pilots proposed, pilots signed.
- Kill the weakest channel, double the best one. Write the three most common objections into STATE.md.

Compliance (Spam Act 2003 and calling rules; verify with ACMA before relying on this):
- Email, SMS and likely social-media DMs sent to promote services are "commercial electronic messages". They need (a) consent, express or reasonably inferred, (b) clear sender identification, and (c) a functional unsubscribe. Inferred consent from a published business address only holds when the message is relevant to the recipient's role and the address carries no no-marketing notice.
- Replies to someone who contacted you first are not cold outreach. Keep a note of the source and date of every contact.
- Do not buy lists or scrape addresses with software.
- Telemarketing: wash numbers against the Do Not Call Register; check ACMA's permitted calling hours (weekdays and Saturday windows, none on Sundays or public holidays); identify yourself and the business at the start; end the call if asked; record opt-outs.
- Use real names, real business name, real phone. No fake urgency, no invented clients, no claims about outcomes. Call recording needs consent under WA law; do not record cold calls.

## 4. Ready-to-send scripts

Replace `[ ]` fields. Keep every claim to what Good'Ai actually does today.

### Script 1 — Cold DM (social or LinkedIn, after connect/accept) — 1a

> Hi [Name], Kevin here from Good'Ai in Perth. I set up phone-answering agents for small trade businesses so missed calls get captured while the team is on the job.
> No pitch if it's not useful. Do you ever lose enquiries because nobody can pick up? If so, I'm happy to give you a free 20-minute look at it and write up what I find.
> If not interested, just say and I won't message again.

**1b — Business email version (Spam Act footer)**

> Subject: Missed calls at [Business] — quick question
>
> Hi [Name],
> I found [Business] via [Google Maps / your website]. I'm Kevin, I run Good'Ai in Perth, where we set up AI phone answering and admin automation for small businesses.
> If calls go unanswered while you're on a job, I can show you how it could be handled and give you a free written review of where the admin time goes. You can hear the demo line now on (08) 7741 4191.
> Would a 20-minute chat this week suit?
>
> Kevin [Surname], Good'Ai, Perth WA [business address/ABN]
> You're receiving this because [Business]'s contact details are published for business enquiries. If you'd prefer no further emails, reply "stop" or click [unsubscribe link] and I'll remove you.

### Script 2 — Cold call opener

> "Hi, is this [Name]? It's Kevin from Good'Ai here in Perth. I'm calling a few local [plumbers / sparkies] about missed calls. Is now a bad time? ... [Pause.] I'll be quick: when you're on a job and the phone rings, what happens to that call?"
> [Listen.]
> "We set up a phone assistant that picks up, takes the details and sends you a message. I'm not trying to sign you today. Could I send you the demo number so you can ring it and judge for yourself? What's the best text or email?"
>
> If no / not interested: "No problem, thanks for your time. I'll take you off my list." Record it.
> If "how did you get my number": "From your public business listing, [Google Maps]. I can remove it now if you like."

### Script 3 — Follow-up (send day 3, then day 8, then stop)

> **Day 3:** Hi [Name], Kevin from Good'Ai following up on my note about missed calls. If it's useful, the demo line is (08) 7741 4191, ring it any time. Happy to do the free 20-minute look whenever suits.
> **Day 8:** Last one from me, [Name]. If missed enquiries aren't a problem at [Business], no worries at all, and I'll stop here. If they are, I'm at [number/email] and can book a time this week.
> **After a walk-in:** "Good chatting to you at [place] today. I've written the demo number below, ring it from your mobile and see what it sounds like: (08) 7741 4191. Kevin, Good'Ai."

### Script 4 — Free opportunity-audit pitch (the call/meeting itself)

> "Thanks for the time, [Name]. I'll keep this to 20 minutes. I'm not selling a package. I'm going to ask how a normal week runs, where calls, quotes and admin get dropped, and tell you honestly what's worth fixing and what to leave alone. Afterwards you get a one-page written summary either way.
> So: walk me through what happens from the moment a customer first calls to the moment you send an invoice. Where does it stick? How do you know when a quote hasn't been answered?"
> [Listen. Note two or three concrete problems in their own words.]
> "Here's what I'd do first, and what I'd leave alone. If it makes sense, a pilot is [scope in one sentence]. If it doesn't, you keep the summary."
> Close: "Would you like me to scope the pilot, or sit on it for a week?" No pressure; note the answer.

Proof stance for the call: "We're new. I don't have client results yet, and I won't invent any. What I can do is show you the demo and be straight about what it can and can't do."

### Script 5 — Referral ask

> Hi [Name], thanks again for your time. Is there one other business owner you know who loses calls when the team's on the job? If so, would you be happy to introduce us, or pass on my details? No obligation either way.

### Script 6 — Facebook group post (only where promotion is allowed)

> Perth tradies and small business owners: how many calls do you miss in a normal week, and what do you do about them?
> I'm Kevin, I run Good'Ai in Perth. We set up AI phone answering and quote/follow-up automation. I'm offering free 20-minute reviews to a handful of local owners to see where the admin time goes, with a one-page write-up either way. Comment or message if you'd like one. Demo line to try: (08) 7741 4191.
